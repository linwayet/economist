1. 文章摘要 Article Summary

English Summary: This article examines whether employers should care about the length of employees’ commutes. It begins from the obvious dislike of commuting: most people see travel between home and work as neither relaxing nor productive, and surveys show that they generally want shorter journeys. Research cited in the article links longer commutes to higher staff turnover among teachers and lower patent output after corporate relocations. Yet the article also stresses that commuting is not purely a cost. People may tolerate longer travel in exchange for cheaper housing, better local amenities and a wider choice of employers. Commuting can also serve as a psychological transition between work and home, especially when active travel is involved. The article concludes that firms can help through subsidies, hybrid work and flexible hours, but the largest levers belong to policymakers through public transport and housing supply. The strongest warning sign for employers is sudden preference change, especially among new mothers.

中文摘要：本文探討企業是否應該在意員工通勤時間。文章先指出，多數人不會希望人生花更多時間通勤，因為往返住家與職場通常既不放鬆，也不具生產力；調查也顯示，人們普遍想把通勤時間縮短。不過，文章引用多項研究說明，長通勤不只是員工個人的不便，也可能提高教師轉校機率、降低企業搬遷後發明人的專利產出。但作者也提醒，通勤並非只有成本。有人願意忍受較長通勤，換取較低房價、較好的住家附近生活機能，以及更多雇主選擇。通勤也可能成為工作與家庭之間的心理轉換空間。企業可透過補貼、混合辦公與彈性工時減輕負擔，但真正能改善通勤時間的最大力量，仍在大眾運輸與住房供給等公共政策。對雇主而言，最值得注意的是偏好突然改變，尤其是剛生小孩的女性員工。

為何重要：文章用專利產出下降的研究來說明，通勤可能影響創新與工作成果，不只是心情問題。

透過更好的大眾運輸與更多住房供給來改善通勤時間，最大權力掌握在政策制定者手中。

為何重要：這句劃清企業與政府責任，指出通勤問題本質上也與城市規劃和住房政策有關。

3. 完整中文翻譯 Full Article Translation

問一個人有沒有什麼人生遺憾，很少人會說：「我真希望自己這輩子花更多時間通勤。」從家裡到公司，再從公司回家，這段路通常既不放鬆，也不算有生產力。它多半只是例行公事，有時還相當不舒服：只要牽涉到大塞車，或在擁擠車廂裡聞到別人的腋下味，就很難讓人喜歡。

在流行文化裡，通勤的單調感正是重點。通勤可以變成陷阱，像《Exit 8》；也可以變成觀察真正情節的窗口，像《The Girl on the Train》；甚至可以變成對現代社會太過安逸的一種評論，像《The War of the Worlds》。covid-19 疫情讓人們有機會體驗沒有通勤的人生，很多人非常喜歡。

所以，當人們被問到理想通勤時間時，毫不意外，答案通常是：「短一點。」全球來看，人們平均每天大約花一小時通勤。但不管實際通勤時間有多長，他們都想把總時間大約砍半。

University College London 的 Jonas De Vos 去年發表一項研究，調查該校 2,000 多名學生與教職員的通勤偏好，結果相當典型。實際平均單程通勤時間高達 54 分鐘；理想平均時間則是 31 分鐘。（理想值會依交通方式而不同：例如，搭火車一小時的人，所想像的理想通勤時間，會比騎腳踏車 30 分鐘的人更長。）

長途通勤不只讓通勤者付出代價，也會讓雇主付出成本。舉例來說，它可能提高員工流動率。Brown University 的 Francisco Santelli 與 Vanderbilt University 的 Jason Grissom 有一篇論文，研究 Tennessee 州 Nashville 公立學校教師的通勤時間。他們發現，單程通勤時間每增加五分鐘，就預測教師轉到同學區另一所學校的機率增加 0.8 到 1 個百分點。

花在交通上的時間，也可能傷害生產力。Bank of Canada 的 Hongyu Xiao 與共同作者研究發現，企業搬遷造成發明人的通勤距離每增加 10 公里，就與專利數量下降 5% 有關。這個下降背後真正的原因並不清楚，但有一種解釋很簡單：人們花在工作上的時間變少了。

那麼，沒有人會渴望把人生更多時間花在通勤上。不過，即使是長途通勤，也可能有好處。以 De Vos 先生的研究為例，他也問受訪者：他們願意忍受到多長的最大通勤時間？平均來看，這個時間比他們目前的通勤時間還長。這顯示人們多少意識到忍受長通勤的好處：較低住房成本、住家附近較好的生活機能，以及更多雇主選擇。

Copenhagen Business School 的 Ismir Mulalic 指出，儘管交通科技出現巨大變化，全球平均一小時通勤時間多年來仍相當穩定。他想起自己共同撰寫的一項企業搬遷研究；令他驚訝的是，有些突然得到較短通勤時間的員工，面對這份「美麗的禮物」，反而搬家到更遠的地方。

通勤本身也可能帶來回報。有些學者會把通勤說成「liminal space」，也就是一種過渡空間；這個詞有點浮誇，但確實抓住了某個真實面向：往返辦公室的路程，是一種方便的方式，讓人為工作日做好準備，或在下班後慢慢放鬆。像騎腳踏車、走路這類主動移動方式，和比坐在車裡更高的幸福感有關。

企業不該對員工上班路程漠不關心。許多公司提供明示或隱性的補貼，來抵銷通勤成本，從金錢協助到停車位都有。混合辦公與彈性工時也能減輕通勤負擔。不過，真正最有力量改善通勤時間的，是政策制定者，透過更好的大眾運輸與更多住房供給來做到。至於通勤時間在多大程度上反映個人偏好，企業最應該注意的，是那些偏好突然改變的時候。

這特別指向一件事。Mulalic 先生共同撰寫的一篇論文，分析 Denmark 2003 到 2013 年整體工作人口資料後發現，通勤時間長的女性，在生小孩後換工作的機率高出好幾倍。（男性則不是。）每個人都說自己想要更短通勤。新手媽媽是真的如此。

The Economist 是英國創辦的國際新聞與評論刊物，以經濟、政治、商業與國際關係分析聞名。本文延續其常見寫法：從日常現象切入，再用研究數據與政策視角擴大問題。

University College London 是英國倫敦的大型研究型大學，本文引用 Jonas De Vos 對 2,000 多名學生與教職員通勤偏好的研究，用來說明實際通勤與理想通勤之間的落差。

Brown University 與 Vanderbilt University 皆為美國知名研究型大學。文中 Francisco Santelli 與 Jason Grissom 的研究聚焦 Nashville 公立學校教師，指出單程通勤每增加五分鐘，轉校機率增加 0.8 到 1 個百分點。

Bank of Canada 是加拿大中央銀行，負責貨幣政策、金融穩定與經濟研究。文中 Hongyu Xiao 與共同作者把企業搬遷、通勤距離與專利產出連在一起分析。

Copenhagen Business School 是丹麥重要商學院。Ismir Mulalic 研究通勤、搬遷與勞動市場選擇，本文引用他對全球平均通勤時間穩定性的觀察。

Key Economic & Business Concepts

Staff turnover 指員工離開原職位、轉到其他單位或離職的流動情況。對企業而言，高流動率通常代表招募、訓練與組織知識流失成本上升；本文把長通勤視為可能推動流動率的因素。

Productivity 在本文不只是「工作時間長短」，而是投入時間後產出的價值。專利下降 5% 的研究提醒管理者，地點決策與搬遷可能透過通勤時間影響創新。

Housing supply 是通勤問題的核心之一。若城市中心住房不足或太貴，員工會搬到更遠的地方，用時間換取房價、空間或生活機能。

Hybrid working 與 flexible hours 是企業能直接運用的工具。它們不能單獨解決交通與房市結構問題，但能降低尖峰通勤壓力與員工生活衝突。

Geopolitical & Policy Context

本文主要不是地緣政治文章，而是城市政策與勞動市場分析。它把通勤放在公共運輸、住房供給、企業選址與家庭照護責任之間，說明「上班要花多久」其實是都市制度設計的結果。

美國 Nashville 教師研究與 Denmark 全體工作人口資料，分別代表地方公共服務勞動市場與全國性人口資料分析。兩者共同指向：通勤時間會影響人們是否留在原工作，但效果會因職業、性別與家庭事件而不同。

Key People & Figures

Jonas De Vos：University College London 研究者，本文引用其 2025 年研究，指出受訪者實際平均單程通勤 54 分鐘，理想平均 31 分鐘。

Francisco Santelli 與 Jason Grissom：研究 Nashville 公立學校教師通勤與校內轉任機率，提供雇主成本的實證例子。

Hongyu Xiao：Bank of Canada 研究者，與共同作者研究企業搬遷後通勤距離增加與專利產出下降的關聯。

Ismir Mulalic：Copenhagen Business School 學者，本文引用其通勤與企業搬遷研究，以及 Denmark 2003-2013 年工作人口資料。個別論文細節如樣本處理與模型設定，此資訊待查證。

Connecting Dots

本文的核心不是簡單說「通勤越短越好」。它真正連起來的是三組取捨：員工在時間、住房與雇主選擇之間取捨；企業在辦公地點、留才與補貼之間取捨；政府在運輸投資與住房供給之間取捨。當人生事件改變偏好，像新手媽媽面對照護責任，原本可忍受的通勤就可能變成換工作的導火線。

7. 原文全文 Original Article（關鍵單字標記版）

Ask someone if they have regrets, and very few people will say: “I wish I had spent more of my life commuting.” The time spent travelling from home to work and back again tends to be neither relaxing nor productive. It is usually routine and sometimes unpleasant: anything that involves loads of traffic or armpits is hard to like.

In popular culture the monotony is the point. Commuting becomes a trap (“Exit 8”), a window on the real action (“The Girl on the Train”) or a commentary on the softness of modern society (“The War of the Worlds”). The covid-19 pandemic gave people the chance to experience a commute-free existence, and many of them loved it.

So when people are surveyed about their perfect commute time, the unsurprising answer tends to be “shorter”. On average people around the world spend roughly an hour a day commuting. But however long their travel time is, they want to lop that total roughly in half.

A study published last year by Jonas De Vos of University College London into the commuting preferences of over 2,000 students and staff at the university is typical. The average actual commute time was a hefty 54 minutes one way; the average ideal time was 31 minutes. (Ideals vary depending on how someone gets to work: a person who is on a train for an hour, for example, envisages a commute that is much longer than someone with a 30-minute bike ride.)

Long journeys can impose costs on employers as well as commuters. For instance, they can increase staff turnover. A paper by Francisco Santelli of Brown University and Jason Grissom of Vanderbilt University examines the commute times for teachers at public schools serving Nashville, Tennessee. They find that each five-minute increase in one-way commute time predicts a 0.8-1 percentage-point increase in the probability that a teacher will transfer to another school within the district.

Time spent travelling can also hurt productivity. A study by Hongyu Xiao of the Bank of Canada and his co-authors found that every 10km increase in an inventor’s commuting distance brought about by a corporate relocation was associated with a 5% decrease in patents. What lies behind this fall-off is not clear, but one explanation is simply that people are spending less time at work.

No one yearns to spend more of their life commuting, then. But even long journeys can have benefits. In his study, for example, Mr De Vos also asked people what was the maximum commute length they were prepared to tolerate. This was more, on average, than the length of their current commute, which suggests some awareness of the upsides to tolerating protracted travel times: lower housing costs, better amenities close to home and a greater choice of employer.

Ismir Mulalic of Copenhagen Business School points out that the one-hour global average commute has remained pretty steady over time despite big changes in transport technology. He recalls a study he co-authored into the effects of a corporate relocation; to his surprise, some of the workers who were suddenly handed shorter commutes reacted to this “beautiful present” by moving home to be farther away.

A commute can bring rewards of its own, too. Some scholars burble about commuting as “a liminal space”, a pompous term that nonetheless captures something real: the journey to and from the office is a handy way to gear up for the working day or to wind down from it. Active modes of travel like cycling and walking are correlated with greater well-being than sitting in a car.

Firms are right not to be indifferent to their employees’ journeys to work. Plenty offer explicit and implicit subsidies to defray commuting costs, from financial help to parking spaces. Hybrid working and flexible hours can also ease the burden of travelling. But the greatest power to improve commute times, through better public transport and more housing supply, lies in the hands of policymakers. And to the extent that commuting time is an expression of individual preferences, firms should pay most attention when those preferences suddenly change.

That means one event in particular. A paper co-authored by Mr Mulalic which looked at data for the full working population of Denmark between 2003 and 2013 found that women with a long commute are several times more likely to change jobs when they have a child. (Men are not.) Everyone says they want a shorter commute. New mothers mean it. ■
